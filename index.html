Brainlet — privacy policy

Effective date: 19 November 2026

Brainlet is a memory app. You type a thought, it is written down, and later you can ask for it back. This policy says exactly what happens to what you write.

The short version: your notes live on your phone. Nothing leaves it unless you ask, and the one time a note can leave — sending it to an AI provider you chose — Brainlet asks first.

What is stored, and where

Your notes, the people and things they mention, the times they refer to, the tasks they create, calendar proposals and the search index built from them are stored in a single database on your phone. There is no Brainlet account, no Brainlet server, and no copy of your notes anywhere else unless you make one yourself.

API keys you enter for an AI provider are encrypted with a key held in Android's Keystore. They are never included in any backup.

Work that happens on your phone

  • Understanding notes. Notes are read by rules built into the app and, if you download one, by a language model that runs on the phone. Neither sends anything anywhere.
  • Search by meaning. To find notes that mean the same thing in different words, Brainlet turns each note into a list of numbers (an “embedding”) with a small model that runs on the phone. Those numbers stay in the same on-phone database as the notes.
  • Answers. When you ask a question, the answer is built from your own notes, on the phone, and shows the notes it came from. With Brainlet Pro, the language model on the phone also writes a short answer to your exact question from the notes it found. That happens on the phone too, and nothing is sent anywhere.

What leaves your phone

Nothing, unless you ask for it. These are the only cases:

  1. Sending one note to an AI provider — optional, and only with your consent. If you add your own key for OpenRouter, OpenAI or DeepSeek in Settings, you can choose to have one note read by that provider. The first time you do this for a provider, Brainlet shows a dialog naming it and explaining that the note's text will leave the phone and be handled under that provider's own terms and privacy policy. Nothing is sent unless you agree. After that, the text of the note you chose and the time you wrote it go to that provider, over HTTPS, under your own account. No other note is sent, and nothing is sent in the background. You can withdraw consent in Settings at any time; deleting the key withdraws it too. If a request fails, the note is still saved and read on the phone instead.
  2. Downloading an on-device model — no notes involved. If you choose to download a model in Settings, Brainlet fetches the model file from Hugging Face. That request asks for a file; it contains none of your notes. As with any download, Hugging Face sees your device's IP address. Downloads use Wi-Fi only.
  3. Calendar. When a note mentions a time, Brainlet may offer to add it to your calendar. It asks for calendar permission only the first time you tap Add. Once you have allowed it, Brainlet reads your calendars on the phone for two things: to find one it may write to, and, when it offers an event, to look for a similar event already there so it does not suggest a duplicate. It writes an event only when you tap Add, and only that one event, using Android's calendar provider. Nothing read from your calendar leaves the phone; your calendar is synced by whichever account owns it, not by Brainlet.
  4. Google Tasks (optional, off by default). If you turn on Google Tasks in Settings, Google shows its own screen asking you to let Brainlet manage your tasks. After that, each time you tap Add on a calendar suggestion, Brainlet sends that one item to the default task list in your Google account: its title, its date, its time, and the text of the note it came from. Brainlet sends it directly from your phone to Google; there is no Brainlet server in between. Brainlet does not read, change or delete your other tasks, and it sends nothing for items you did not confirm. Turn the switch off to stop sending at once, and remove Brainlet's access in your Google Account under Security → Third-party connections. Tasks already sent stay in Google Tasks until you delete them there. Brainlet's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
  5. Brainlet Pro and tips. Brainlet is free to use. Free use includes up to 20 AI-assisted actions a day, and anything that does not need AI is unlimited. Brainlet Pro is an optional one-time purchase that removes the daily AI limit and adds features such as answers written for your exact question. Tips are optional and unlock nothing. Google Play processes every payment. Brainlet never sees your payment details. It learns only whether a purchase went through, and it asks Google Play whether your account owns Pro so that Pro returns after a reinstall. The count of today's AI actions is kept only on your phone.
  6. Pictures. When you attach a picture to a note, you pick it with Android's photo picker, which gives Brainlet only the pictures you choose; Brainlet needs no access to your photo library. A smaller copy is stored in Brainlet's private storage on your phone. Pictures are not uploaded and not included in backups. Deleting the note deletes its pictures. With Brainlet Pro, Brainlet reads the words in each picture on your phone (Google ML Kit, which runs on the device) and, if you have downloaded the on-device Gemma model, writes a short description on your phone too. Only if you have not downloaded that model, and you have added your own OpenAI or OpenRouter key and agreed to send notes to that provider, is a smaller copy of the picture sent to that provider to be described. The words and description are stored with the note so you can search for them.
  7. Meeting transcripts (Brainlet Pro). When you choose Read a meeting and paste or open a transcript, Brainlet reads it with the model you pick on that screen: your phone's own model (nothing leaves the phone) or a provider you have added a key for and agreed to send notes to (then the transcript is sent to that provider). Brainlet shows you the action items and notes it found, and saves only what you approve. The transcript itself is not stored. If you enter your name to help Brainlet find your own tasks, it is kept only on your phone, and it is included in what is sent only when you choose a provider. If you open a voice memo there, the recording is turned into text on your phone by the Gemma model; the audio is never uploaded or stored by Brainlet, and the text is then handled like any transcript.
  8. Backups. When you choose Back up notes, Brainlet writes a file to the place you pick. If you pick a cloud folder, that provider stores it under your account. Brainlet does not upload anything itself.
  9. Reporting an answer. If you tap Report this answer, Brainlet opens your own email app with the question and answer filled in, addressed to the contact below. Nothing is sent unless you press Send, and you can edit or delete any of it first.

Brainlet contains no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no tracking of any kind. Android's own cloud backup is turned off for this app, so your notes are not copied to Google's servers by the operating system either.

The home-screen widget

The widget reads the same on-phone database to show the single nearest thing that is due, and, once a task's date has passed, asks whether it is done. It displays what is already on your phone; it sends nothing anywhere.

What is not collected

Brainlet does not collect your name, email address, contacts, location, device identifiers or usage statistics. It has no servers to send them to.

Your control over it

  • Delete a note and it disappears from the library, from search and from answers. A small record that the note was deleted (without its text) is kept, so that restoring a backup cannot bring it back.
  • Withdraw cloud consent for a provider in Settings, or delete its key. Brainlet will ask again before any later send. A note already sent to a provider is held by that provider under its terms; Brainlet cannot delete it there, so contact the provider.
  • Remove a downloaded model in Settings.
  • Uninstall the app and Android deletes the database, the keys and everything else Brainlet stored on the phone.
  • Backups are files you own, in a place you chose. Deleting the file deletes the backup.

Children

Brainlet is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone.
